              Functional Movement Science (HAS4867)
              Measurement of Range of Motion (ROM)

Range of Motion can be defined as the amount of motion available at a joint.
Each specific joint has a normal range of motion that is expressed in degrees.
Universal goniometer is commonly used for measuring ROM.


Universal Goniometer
A universal goniometer is made of metal or plastic and it has three parts:
1. Body
2. Stationary arm
3. Moving arm




The body of the goniometer is like a protractor and may form a full or half circle,
with the scale from 0 - 180° or 360°. The axis is at the center of the body that
allows the moving arm to move freely on the body of the device.

The axis and body is placed over the joint being measured.

The stationary arm will be aligned with the inactive part of the joint measured
while the moving arm is placed on the part of the limb, which is moved in the
joint’s motion.


Shoulder Joint

1. Shoulder flexion

Subject Test Position
Subject in sitting, with back straight and supported
Goniometer Alignment
1. Axis – center of humeral head near acromion process
2. Stationary arm – parallel mid-axillary line
3. Moving arm – aligned with midline of humerus (lateral epicondyle)
Action
Ask the subject to raise arm up

2. Shoulder Extension

Subject Test Position
Subject in sitting, with back straight and supported

3. Shoulder Abduction

Subject test position
• Subject in sitting, with back straight and supported
• Shoulder in external rotated position
Goniometer Alignment
1. Axis – center of humeral head, anterior portion of acromion process
2. Stationary arm – parallel to sternum
3. Moving arm – aligned with midline of humerus
Action
Ask the subject to abduct the shoulder

4. Shoulder Internal Rotation

Subject Test Position
• Subject Supine
• Shoulder 90° abduction
• Forearm neutral
• Elbow fixed 90°
• Stabilize arm
Goniometer Alignment
• Axis – olecranon process of ulna
• Stationary arm – aligned vertically
• Moving arm – aligned with ulna (styloid process)
Action
Ask the subject to rotate the arm down to the direction of the feet

5. Shoulder External Rotation

Forearm

• Subject sitting
• Shoulder neutral
• Elbow fixed at 90°
• Palm in neutral position
Goniometer Alignment
• Axis – lateral to ulnar styloid process
• Stationary arm – parallel to humerus
• Moving arm – aligned with dorsal aspect of wrist
Action
Ask the subject to pronate the forearm

2. Supination

Subject Test Position
• Subject sitting
• Shoulder neutral
• Elbow fixed at 90°
• Hold a pen in the fist at neutral position
Goniometer Alignment
• Axis – medial to ulnar styloid
• Stationary arm – parallel to humerus
• Moving arm – aligned with the pen
Action
Ask the subject to supinate the forearm

Fingers

1. Metacarpophalangeal Joint Flexion (MCP flexion)

Subject Test Position
• Subject sitting with forearm resting on table
• Wrist and interphalangeal joints relaxed
• Forearm neutral

Goniometer Alignment
• Place the finger goniometer over the Dorsal
  MCP joint
• Stationary arm - aligned with metacarpal
• Moving arm – aligned with proximal phalange
Action
Ask the subject to flex the MCP joint
