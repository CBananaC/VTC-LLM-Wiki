                 FOREWORD, THIRD EDITION
    It is indeed an honor and a pleasure to write the foreward to the third
edition of Special Tests for Orthopedic Examination.
    The goal in writing this textbook was to provide a pocket guide for
examination of patients/athletes with orthopedic injuries; this goal has
been surpassed. This guide offers a complete overview of critical ortho-
pedic exams which is necessary to make a diagnosis in an increasingly
expanding field. It offers a simple, yet thorough collection of important
information necessary for athletic trainers, physical therapists, occupa-
tional therapists, physician assistants, and physicians entering the sports
medicine field. In these days of high tech imaging, some clinicians seem
to forget the importance of a good exam—often to their detriment. We
must always go back to the history and physical examination to make
the diagnosis and initiate treatment.
    So... what's new in this third edition and why should you buy it
(especially if you have an earlier edition)? The answer is that the authors
have really "stepped it up" this go-round. The new edition features an
evidence-based approach including current and historical references.
The illustrations have been expanded and arrows have been added to
better demonstrate each examination. Finally the authors have added
some new, previously undescribed examinations that you can try on
your own!
    In sum, Special Tests for Orthopedic Examination, 3rd edition, is an
excellent text that belongs on your bookshelf (or in your pocket!).

   Mark D. Miller, MD
   Professor of Orthopaedic Surgery
   Head, Division of Sports Medicine
   University of Virginia
                  FOREWORD, SECOND EDITION
    Dr. Craig Denegar correctly noted in the foreword to the first edition of
this work that “The physical examination is a critical component of any ortho-
pedic evaluation process.” The variety of tests and measurements used to deter-
mine the status of a patient's strength, flexibility, and range of motion contin-
ue to proliferate. This creates the need to conduct numerous special tests to
determine if the diagnosis made from observation and subjective information
can be pinpointed and confirmed or denied. All of these special tests have been
developed to isolate specific structures during the evaluation process.
    This second edition of Special Tests for Orthopedic Examination is a

the evaluation process. Special tests are essentially designed to isolate spe-
cific structures during the evaluation process.
    Special Tests for Orthopedic Examination is a unique and valuable ref-

physicians, and other health care professionals who provide care for those
individuals with orthopedic injuries. Featuring special orthopedic examina-

they have learned in the classroom to clinical and field experiences.

                     PREFACE, THIRD EDITION
    Special Tests for Orthopedic Examination began as a simple concept to
centrally locate commonly used orthopedic tests into a single pocket-type
manual. Through the first two editions, modifications were made to the
design and format of the book, and the manner in which the content was
delivered has been enhanced.
    User-friendly bindings, such as the spiral bound change that was made
to the second edition, have been viewed positively. This has allowed for the
reader to practice a special test while the book remains lying open to the
page of that particular test. Owners of both editions were relieved to not
have pages fall out of the binding from the prying open of the book when
keeping it flat for viewing.
    The addition of references associated with each special test versus a list
of those identified at the conclusion of the text also strengthened the sec-
ond edition. This allowed for the reader to specifically locate information
related to the test being described. The addition of some special tests to the
second edition expanded the reader's opportunity to learn more of the
approaches associated with the orthopedic examination.
    The popularity of the second edition spawned the publication of the text
into Korean and Spanish translations. Nonetheless, anything can be
improved upon. When one is satisfied and becomes complacent, it doesn't
take long to realize that others shift gears and attempt to pass you by. Thus,
refueling has taken place after a brief stint in pit road, and the third edition
has been launched.
    In planning goals for an updated edition, we wanted to keep the basic
original principles of the book intact. Clear images and simplistic, yet
detailed, descriptions serve as the foundation of the book. The opportuni-
ty to enhance upon these principles formed the basis of discussion as we set
forth to create a third edition.
    This edition features a number of changes that we think will please the
reader. First and foremost, tests were edited to provide what we feel is an
accumulation of currently used tests. Some tests were deleted, and some
new ones were added. Tests that were deleted were not felt to be used, not
felt to be practical, not felt to be reliable, and not felt to be valid. This was
based upon our opinion, readers' feedback, and the lack of evidence in the
literature to support the test. Tests that were added were done so based
upon reader feedback and editor consensus, suggesting that these tests have
gained in popularity. While some may not have considerable (or even any)
published evidence to support their effectiveness, clinical discussion has
favored their empirical use.
    In expanding upon our efforts in previous editions to provide peer-
reviewed literature with each test, we have performed an exhaustive search

of the literature to include as much evidence as possible related to research
performed for each test. As was in the past, we continue to believe that it
is not our job as editors to inform the reader as to whether or not a test is
effective, but rather to provide information to allow the reader to make his
or her own interpretation. In addition to listing evidence-based peer
reviewed manuscripts that evaluate the effectiveness of each test, at times
we include manuscripts that simply assist the reader in knowing more
about the historical, anatomical, or even comparative issues related to a
test. And, as was the case in the past editions, there are some tests that cli-
nicians continue to use on a regular basis that simply do not have any peer-
reviewed evidence-based studies to refer to. Once again, we challenge the
readers to come to their own conclusions, and perhaps formulate their own
research to support or refute such a test.
    To assist with taking a two-dimensional photo and having it demon-
strate how one performs a three-dimensional movement, we have added
arrow overlays to many photos. We believe that this will clearly help the
reader identify with directional movements of forces that are applied dur-
ing the movement of certain special tests.
    One of the most frustrating challenges we have faced both in writing
this text and in talking with many clinicians is the fact that there are
numerous special tests used on a daily basis that are found to be helpful to
a clinician, yet there are no published studies to support their efficacy.
Clinicians will continue to use such tests if they provide clinical outcomes
regardless of whether or not a study "statistically proves" a test works.
Similarly, many of us perform techniques on a daily basis that help us prob-
lem solve and differentially diagnose. True to this phenomenon, we have
added a section titled "Contemporary Special Tests". While we clearly rec-
ognize that we are unable to provide a single peer-reviewed manuscript to
justify the reliability and validity of theses tests, we have personally found
them to be helpful to us for the very reasons mentioned previously.
Therefore, we offer to you some "new" special tests that can be tried in
your setting. You may find them to be helpful, you may not. You may even
be stimulated enough to initiate your own research to validate these tests,
at which time we would be happy to reference your work.
     In summary, the third edition has been a fun project. Once the ink start-
ed flowing, ideas began generating. As always, we remind you that special
tests are merely a piece of the puzzle that assist in the evaluation process.
While you piece your puzzles together, we hope that this new edition will
provide you with a handy tool for problem solving.

            INTRODUCTION, THIRD EDITION
SPECIAL Tests:      A Key CLINICAL SKILL
    Webster defines the word "special" as "distinguished by some unusu-
al quality" and "designed for a particular purpose or occasion", and the
word "test" as "a critical examination, observation or evaluation".
    In any orthopaedic evaluation process, the use of special tests assists
to provide critical information leading to, confirming, ruling out, and
monitoring the status of a particular diagnostic condition. It is no won-
der then that in the day of diagnostic imaging and advanced technolog-
ical interventions that clinicians often resort to the basic skills of manu-
al examination for a sense of comfort during the examination process.
    As stated in the previous two editions of this text, special tests are
merely one part of the evaluation process that also relies heavily on his-
tory taking and symptoms, amongst many other components. Yet in the
algorithmic approach to determining what musculoskeletal and/or neu-
rological structures may or may not have been damaged, key special
tests can often come in handy.
    The compilation of the tests in this book reflect a number of ways of
thinking. First, tests included are ones that the editors have seen com-
monly documented and described in numerous orthopaedic and sports
medicine related textbooks. They may not necessarily been proven to
be reliable and/or valid in peer-review studies, yet they continually
seem to be published and taught in curriculums. This may not seem to
jive with the current agenda of evidenced-based medicine, yet how
could one challenge a clinician who regularly uses a particular special
test and has accuracy in diagnostic decision-making and treatment
intervention that is partially a result of the special test and its findings
or lack thereof? Given this fact, we will continue to include such tests
in hope that either one day such a test would be proven valid and/or
reliable, or perhaps we as individually talented clinicians within our
own rights will come to realize that it may in fact not be appropriate to
label a test in such simple terms as “yes, it works” or “no, it doesn't
work”. After all, part of evidence-based medicine is also to determine
the “how's” and “when's”. How do swelling, pain, and range of motion
limitations influence the effectiveness of a special test? How does the
experience of the examining clinician influence the findings? What
about the clinician's hand size, height, setting, and even bedside man-
ners? The simple message here is that regardless of how much evidence
is reviewed and which way the evidence points, some special tests may
or may not work in the hands of different individuals.
Xx      Introduction, Third Edition



    The second important point to make regarding the usage of this text
is that the editors intentionally did not annotate nor summarize refer-
enced manuscript findings for each special test. Primarily based upon
the justification made in the previous paragraph, it is our belief that each
clinician should judge for oneself as to whether or not a particular test
is found to be useful in certain circumstances within one's own ortho-
pedic assessment. The request to provide such information in this text
has been perhaps the most often cited suggestion for future editions.
However, our editing of this text does not render us an authority of spe-
cial tests, but rather a provider of information. As one knows, peer-
reviewed manuscripts can be discussed in forums of professionals who
do not agree on the interpretation of the manuscript, the statistic used,
and sometimes even the accuracy of the conclusions. Performing a crit-
ical analysis of each manuscript listed as a reference is beyond the goal
of this book. The reader will actually note that some references are not
peer-reviewed, but instead serve more as historical documents providing
the very first reading of the proposed special test. In some circum-
stances, you will find it very interesting that the method in which a spe-
cial test is performed today is in fact not how it was originally described.
This leads us to our final point.
    This third edition includes a section titled "Contemporary Special
Tests". This new section is a reflection of how health care and especial-
ly orthopedic evaluation evolves over time. In this section, the editors
are proposing a number of new special tests for clinicians to use. No,
 these tests have not yet been critically reviewed nor studied. They have
been used very successfully in the empirical clinical world that we live
 in every day. As with all other special tests, those new or old, those
 proven to be valid and/or reliable and those not, we are sharing with the
 readers some alternative methods to add to the evaluative algorithm.
 Readers are encouraged to try these tests in different circumstances to
 begin to assess their usefulness. Readers are also encouraged to under-
 take more formal critical analysis and research the specificity and sensi-
 tivity of these special tests. Finally, readers are encouraged to share their
 results with other medical and allied health care professionals in profes-
 sionally published and/or presented forums. We, as editors, are fully
 prepared to take the criticism of those who do not believe we should
 take the bold step of proposing such special tests in professional writ-
ings prior to establishing well-designed methodologies and opening up
our findings to expert review. However, we also believe that, in the
words of Dr. Joe Gieck, "At first you do what you're taught, and then
you do what works". Thus, in the spirit of progressive health care and
                                        Introduction, Third Edition    xxi



willingness to accept change, we believe that proposing such new special
tests can serve as a first step for the basis of forming new evidence.
    The addition in this text of arrow overlays will hopefully provide the
reader with a clearer understanding of illustrative interpretation supple-
mental to the written description. We have continued to make changes
based upon suggestions by readers that have helped to make the text more
user-friendly. However, the content contained within should truly serve as
the basis by which the text is judged. By the nature of the sales and the
feedback from academicians, clinicians, and students alike, we believe that
the text has succeeded in meeting its goals and your interest.
    As always, we empower you to be the decision-maker during your
orthopedic assessment and remind you that the special test is merely one
small step within your algorithm but possibly one giant step for your
patient!

TEST POSITIONING

TEST POSITIONING
   The subject lies on the side with the hips and knees extended such
that the test leg is superior to the nontest leg. The examiner stands with
the proximal hand stabilizing the pelvis and the distal hand supporting
the lower leg (Figure 9-6A).

ACTION
    With the pelvis stabilized to prevent rolling, abduct and extend the
test hip in order to position the iliotibial band (ITB) behind the greater
trochanter (Figure 9-6B). Then allow the leg to slowly lower (adduct).

Positive FINDING
    The inability of the leg to adduct and touch the table is indicative of
ITB (particularly the tensor fasciae latae) tightness. The leg will react
like a "springboard" since the leg remains abducted in mid-air (Figure 9-
6C).
SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS/COMMENTS
    It is important to apply a downward force on the ilium near the crest
while allowing the leg to adduct. This will prevent lateral tilting (ie,
inferior movement) of the pelvis on the side of the test leg, which could
give a false negative result. Additionally, it is important to ensure com-
plete relaxation of the hip abductor muscles. It may be helpful to have
the subject actively adduct the test leg into the support hand and then
relax in order to inhibit hip abductor muscle guarding. This test was
originally described by Ober to be performed with the knee flexed.
However, it has been modified because it is believed that a greater
stretch is placed upon the ITB when the knee is in an extended position.
Furthermore, performing this test with the knee in flexion places greater
tension on the femoral nerve, requiring the examiner to be cognizant of
associated neurological complaints.

actively is likely indicative of an extensor tendon disruption (Figure 12-
3B).

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS/COMMENTS
   The examiner should only apply light resistance against active joint
extension for the purposes of assessing any noticeable contraction.
Moderate to maximal resistance is not encouraged as any tendon dis-
ruption may become complicated with increased contractile efforts. If a
tendon disruption is suspected, this test should not be repeated for
demonstration, confirmation, or educational purposes beyond what is
necessary for clinical diagnosis.

Test POSITION
   The patient is seated on a table with knees flexed to 90 degrees. The
involved ankle is placed in neutral dorsiflexion/plantar flexion. The
examiner   stabilizes   the rearfoot   (subtalar joint) with   one   hand   and
grasps the forefoot with the opposite hand (Figure 12-5A). The distal
hand placement should be in the region of the tarsometatarsal joints.

ACTION
    While assuring stabilization of the subtalar joint, the examiner
applies a medial rotatory force (Figure 12-5B) and a lateral rotatory
force (Figure 12-5C) through the midfoot. The test is performed in
order to stress the ligamentous stabilizers of the talocalcaneonavicular
joint, the cuneonavicular joint, the calcaneocuboid joint, the
cuboideonavicular joint, the cuneocuboid joint, the intercuneiform
joint, and the tarsometatarsal joints.

PosiTIVE FINDING
    Pain in the region of the midfoot is a positive finding for a midfoot
sprain. The location of the pain may assist the clinician in identifying
the exact location of the injury with regard to the joints of the midfoot.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS/COMMENTS
    This test may also be performed with the patient in supine or long-
sitting. This test should not be performed if there is obvious deformity
present.
    Midfoot injuries are commonly found in association with lateral
ankle sprains. This is particularly true when the ankle is in a plan-
tarflexed position and the midfoot is forced into a supination (twisting)
motion. Additionally, injuries to the midfoot can result from repeated
trauma, as seen in distance runners. Overuse injuries to the midfoot are
most commonly associated with excessive pronation during gait.
